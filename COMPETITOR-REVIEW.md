# Virtual assistant website competitor review

The site needs to make a service easy to buy and a workflow easy to evaluate. The redesign uses a compact directory, visible examples, and explicit approval boundaries to do both.

| Reference | What the site does well | How this draft applies the idea |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Wing Assistant](https://wingassistant.com/) | Organises a large catalogue by work function and buyer need. | Five consistent playbooks with direct navigation and a six-row scope matrix on each. |
| [Prialto](https://www.prialto.com/virtual-assistant-services) | Makes management and continuity part of the service proposition. | The trust and onboarding pages specify who approves work, how coverage is scoped, and what stays in the handoff record. |
| [BELAY](https://belaysolutions.com/services/assistant/virtual) | Explains delegation through recognisable roles and tasks. | Each playbook starts with a concrete work item, then shows examples, deliverables and the decisions a buyer keeps. |
| [Athena](https://www.athena.com/how-it-works) | Shows the path from intake to a working relationship. | The first workflow, sample review, and ongoing operating rhythm have distinct steps and outputs. |
| [Linear](https://linear.app/) | Uses detailed product examples to explain what a buyer would experience. | An original operations graphic and an interactive workflow lab show the work without implying an app or borrowing a competitor’s visuals. |

Competitor claims are treated as self-reported. The site uses fictional work samples. Pricing, team details, coverage commitments, certifications and case studies require verified inputs before publication.

## Blue theme and industry navigation

The blue direction uses navy for the opening sections, cobalt for primary actions and pale blue for comparison and reading surfaces. The industry pages use a workflow preview beside the opening copy so buyers can see what a handoff produces before reading the scope.

The review compared how providers organise their content. The draft uses those patterns to show its own scope, examples and approval rules.

| Pricing | [Athena’s pricing page](https://www.athena.com/pricing) makes cadence and inclusions easy to compare. | The draft explains quote inputs, proposal terms and when changing workloads warrant a new scope. |
| About Us | [Prialto](https://www.prialto.com/about-us) separates its story from its leadership team. | Trust and Security | [Athena’s security page](https://www.athena.com/security) addresses access and operating practices directly. | The draft shows the permissions a task may need and who should approve broader access. |
